# STRATO BTCS: Reconciliation of the Two Independent Derivations

**Purpose.** Two agents derived the five-layer spherical BTCS scheme independently:

- **Derivation A** ([01_from_ftcs_to_five_layer_btcs.md](01_from_ftcs_to_five_layer_btcs.md) in its first version, with [00_original_documents.md](00_original_documents.md)), written from the owner's original documents;
- **Derivation B** ([archive/independent_derivation_b.md](archive/independent_derivation_b.md)), written from first principles without the owner's documents or derivation A.

This document compares them equation by equation, records every difference, and states how each difference was resolved: by algebra where algebra settles it, and otherwise by an uncommitted scratch experiment. The results are integrated into Document 1 ([01_from_ftcs_to_five_layer_btcs.md](01_from_ftcs_to_five_layer_btcs.md), cited "D1") and Document 2 ([02_final_formulation.md](02_final_formulation.md), cited "D2"). Equation numbers of derivation A are written "A(…)", those of derivation B "B(…)".

**Scratch experiment of the reconciliation.** A short uncommitted program implemented Document 2 row by row: the grid (F1) to (F4), the rows (F11), (F12) and (F17), the initial vector (F21) with (F22), the Thomas algorithm (F24), and the inventory, release and residual (F25) to (F30). It also integrated the semi-discrete system exactly in time by an eigen-decomposition of the symmetrised operator. It used the synthetic five-layer input `inputs/synthetic/synthetic_five_layer.toml` (labelled there as not literature data) and the homogeneous-sphere series solution. Its results are numerical checks of the algebra. They are not a verification of the Rust solver and not experimental validation.

**The owner's documents.** The comparison of derivation B's checks with the owner's handwritten treatment (item 25 below) uses the transcription in document 00. The two PDF documents were not re-read for this reconciliation, because no difference between A and B depended on a passage that document 00 does not transcribe. After independent verification, the transcriptions that the BTCS derivation relies on most were spot-checked against the PDFs (Section 6, item V6).

---

## 1. Summary

The two derivations arrive at the same scheme. Every matrix coefficient, the centre row, the interface rows, the outer boundary row, the release formula, the initial kernel-surface value and the conservation identity agree exactly after a change of notation. The differences are of four kinds:

1. **Notation** (items 1 to 7): resolved by directive Section 5 and by the owner's notation.
2. **Statements that were true in one setting and stated more generally** (items 16 and 14): resolved by algebra, confirmed by experiment, and corrected in D1.
3. **Results that one derivation has and the other lacks** (items 10, 12, 22, 23, 27): checked, and integrated into D1 and D2.
4. **Implementation-dependent numbers** (items 18 and 21): explained; no number is adopted as a target.

No difference revealed an error in the scheme itself.

## 4. Points on which both derivations agree and that were re-checked

The following were derived by both agents and re-checked algebraically in the reconciliation; no difference was found:

- the shell balance and Fick's law leading to the conservative equation with $D$ inside the derivative, and the constant-$D$ form inside each layer;
- the inconsistency of a constant-$D$ stencil at an interface node (a rate of change growing like $1/\Delta r$);
- the face factor $r_ir_{i+1}$ as the exact steady conductance of a spherical shell;
- $L_1 = 0$ and $V_0 = 0$, the one-way coupling of the centre, and the rule that no finite-volume centre weight may enter the inventory;
- the trapezoidal-rule interpretation of the conserved inventory;
- the first-order local error at an interface node, with the flux $q$ cancelling between the two sides and the geometric terms cancelling against the face factors;
- the M-matrix sign pattern and diagonal-dominance margin of at least one, with no grid condition and no time-step condition;
- the instability of marching for $C_{i+1}$ (document 00 and A) and the stability of the Thomas algorithm.
